# Final project
## Only Works in This Room

**Make an interactive immersive work for the Immersive Lab.** Combine it with anything: installation, performance, sound, objects in the room, drawing, video, the 2D or the 3D template, or a form we haven't named. Teams of **3–4**, the same team from week 9 to the end.

**Three rules:**

1. **At least one interaction.** Someone in the room, a visitor or a performer, changes what the room does, live, through a sensor: by being there, moving, touching something, making a sound, or using a phone. Pressing play doesn't count.
2. **It only works in this room.** Four walls, a floor, no ceiling, a door in the left wall, an audience that walks around. Use that. If it would work just as well on a laptop screen, it isn't finished.
3. **It works without you, and without the sensor.** On presentation day it starts cold and keeps going while you stand back. If nobody interacts, or the tracking drops out, the room still does something.

**Sensors in the lab:** webcam, Vive trackers, a RealSense depth camera through your laptop, Arduino, and phones over Wi-Fi. See [Getting data in](immersive-lab/#data). Tracking in the lab is rough: design for **blobs, not skeletons**. Presence, position and movement survive. Fingers don't.

## Bring to your tutorial

1. **Your piece in one sentence.** What happens, and to whom.
2. **A floor plan.** Where things are on the five surfaces, where the audience stands, where they come in.
3. **Your interaction.** What is sensed, what it changes, and what the room does when nobody is there.
4. **Something running in the room.** Rough is fine. Ideas on paper don't count.
5. **One question** you can't answer without the room.

The tutorial happens in the room, so test, don't present.

## References

A mix of old and new, across forms. Most turn a body into input: look at what each does when nobody is there. The last one has no sensor at all. It's here for how far one subject can travel across forms.

| Work | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| Myron Krueger, [*Videoplace*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videoplace) (1970s–) | Your silhouette, live, inside the picture. No goggles, no gloves. The first room that answered a body. (Reading, week 10.) |
| Scott Snibbe, [*Boundary Functions*](https://www.snibbe.com/art/boundaryfunctions) (1998) | Lines on the floor divide the space between whoever is standing there. With one person, nothing happens. A floor piece built entirely on position. |
| Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, [*Pulse Room*](https://www.lozano-hemmer.com/pulse_room.php) (2006) | Hold a sensor and a bulb flashes with your heartbeat. Let go and your pulse moves down the line of up to 300 bulbs. One simple sensor, and the room keeps the last visitors. |
| Chris Milk, *The Treachery of Sanctuary* (2012) | Three panels and your shadow. Birds make it, birds tear it apart, then it grows wings when you flap your arms. A body tracked as a shape, which is what the lab can do. |
| Adrien M & Claire B, [*Hakanaï*](https://www.shift.jp.org/en/archives/2015/09/adrien-m-claire-b.html/2/) (2013) | A dancer inside a cube of live projected images that move with her. After the dance, the audience walks in. A performance that becomes an installation. |
| Lauren Lee McCarthy, [*SOMEONE*](https://get-lauren.net/SOMEONE) (2019) | A human Alexa: gallery visitors watch real homes and control their lights and devices. The "interaction" is people serving people. A room that responds can be uncomfortable. |
| Lap-See Lam, [*Phantom Banquet*](https://www.asymptotejournal.com/visual/the-chinese-restaurant-as-portal-lap-see-lam/) (2019–20) and [*Floating Sea Palace*](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/628242/lap-see-lamfloating-sea-palace) (2024) | Chinese restaurants in Sweden, 3D-scanned, then carried through an app, a 360° VR film with live music, and a shadow-animation film inside a bamboo scaffold structure. One subject, many forms. The 360° film is the 3D template's panorama route; the shadow play is next week's Tanizaki. |

More in the [week 9 lecture](weeks/week09.md) and the [Immersive Lab guide](immersive-lab/).

## Grading

| Weight | What |
|---|---|
| **30%** | **Concept**: is there an idea, and does the room carry it? |
| **30%** | **Interaction and space**: what does someone's presence change, and does the piece use the walls, the floor, the corners, the door? |
| **20%** | **Technical and reliability**: it works cold, without you, and when the sensor fails |
| **20%** | **Presentation and documentation**: the talk, the video, the statement |

You must be able to explain everything in your piece, including any code from Gemini.
