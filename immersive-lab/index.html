Inside the Immersive Lab

Four walls, a floor, and one big picture. This guide explains how the room works, how the lab's two TouchDesigner templates fill it, and what to do before your first 30 minutes inside.

1The room

The lab is a box you stand inside. Projectors cover the four walls and the floor. There is no ceiling: look up and the picture stops. Your own body casts shadows on the floor image. You come in through a door in the left wall, and the door is part of the projected picture.

Drag the model to look around. Then press Show the five cameras: the 3D template draws each surface from one eye in the middle of the room.

The 3D model needs WebGL, which this browser has turned off. The table and diagrams below have the same information.

2One big picture

You never control the projectors one by one. The lab computer sends one tall image, 3840 pixels wide and 5400 high, and the projection system cuts it up and puts each part on its surface. Press Unfold into one picture above to watch the room flatten into that image.

The circle test

In the unfolded picture every circle is round. Fold it back and look at the front wall: the same circle becomes a tall oval. The front and back walls are 3 times wider than they are tall, but their slice of the image is 3.56 times wider. The image gets squeezed sideways to fit, so everything flat on those walls looks about 19% taller.

Fix for flat images on front and backMake them 3840 × 1280: 3 : 1, the wall's real shape. The 2D template squeezes each image into its wall's pixels, so it comes out right on the wall. Side walls and floor are close enough to leave alone. 3D scenes don't need any of this.

One pixel on the wall is about 3 mm. Think big: thin lines and small text vanish.

3The sweet spot

The 3D template draws the whole room as seen from one eye, in the middle of the room, 1.5 m off the floor. From there, a 3D scene looks perfect, and corners disappear. Anywhere else, some things slide out of place. Flat images from the 2D template sit on the walls, so they are right from everywhere.

Which things? Try it. This is the room from above. Drag you around the room, and drag the object (or use the buttons).

Two things are always true. From the sweet spot, everything lines up. And an object placed on a wall lines up from everywhere, because the painted picture and the object are in the same place.

The rule for group workPut what matters on the walls. Use depth for atmosphere.

Your audience will stand all over the room, so anything that floats in mid-air or sits far beyond a wall will only be right for one of them. Corners show it most: a straight line that crosses a corner bends unless you stand at the centre.

The door matters too. Everyone enters through the left wall, about 5 m from the sweet spot, so the first look anyone gets at your piece is from one of the worst places to stand. Press By the door above and try each object position. Decide what a visitor should see in their first three seconds, from there.

Flat images

Treat the room as five screens. The 2D template has one orange slot per surface, named Floor, Front, Back, Left and Right. Wire an image, a video or a live visual into a slot and it fills that surface.

Where
lab-2d.toe, its five slots
Good for
Graphics, text, video, reactive visuals, webcams
Watch out
Make front and back images 3 : 1 (section 2). The upside: flat images look right from anywhere in the room.

A 360° picture

Wrap the room in a 360° photo or video. The template puts it on a giant sphere around the room. Out of the box, it shows the lab's test room: a numbered checkerboard.

Where
lab-3d.toe: geo1 and geo2 (two copies of the same sphere), coloured by phong1 and phong2
Good for
360° photos and video, skies, found footage
Watch out
Soft. A 360° image spreads its pixels all the way round, so the front wall gets about a quarter of its sharpness.

No-glasses mode and 3D-glasses modeThe room starts in no-glasses mode (the lab's manual calls it "2D mode"), and everything on this page works in it, including lab-3d.toe. There is also a version for glasses mode, lab-3d-stereo.toe (stereo means one picture per eye, which the glasses separate). It makes 3D objects really sit in front of or behind the walls. It costs half the sharpness, a pair of glasses for every visitor, and the lab has to switch the room over. Talk to your tutor before planning for it.

5Where things go

For 3D work. Everything in the 3D template is in metres. The centre of the floor is 0, 0, 0. A default Box in TouchDesigner is exactly 1 m on each side, so you can think in real sizes.

TouchDesigner's Translate sets the centre of an object. A 1 m box at 0, 0, 0 is half under the floor; use 0, 0.5, 0 to stand it on the floor. Anything directly above you, higher than the walls, is in the hole where the ceiling would be.

6Your first scene

Do this at home, on your own laptop, before you book the room.

  1. Install the right TouchDesigner

    Install TouchDesigner 2025, the exact build listed on Moodle, so your file opens on the lab computer. Use the Educational licence from your tutor. The free Non-Commercial licence stops at 1280 × 1280 pixels, far too small for this room.

  2. Make your project folder

    Download lab-2d.zip and unzip it to get lab-2d.toe. Make a folder for your project, put the file in it and rename it after your project. Make a media folder next to it.

  3. Open it and look around

    Open the file and double-click project1 to go inside. Five orange boxes are named after the surfaces: Floor, Front, Back, Left, Right. Each one is a slot, already the right size for its surface. The pictures going into them are demos from TouchDesigner's own sample files.

  4. Put your own image on the front wall

    Copy your image into media. In TouchDesigner press Tab, choose TOP → Movie File In, place it and pick your file. Drag a wire from its right edge to the left edge of Front. The new wire replaces the demo. Videos work the same way.

  5. Get the shape right

    Front and back images should be 3840 × 1280 (section 2). Side walls and floor can use their pixel size.

  6. Check the whole picture

    out1 shows exactly what the room receives: floor on top, then front, back, and the two side walls side by side.

  7. Save into your folder

    Keep the .toe and every image, video and sound inside your project folder (section 7).

When your group needs 3D

For 3D objects or a 360° picture, use lab-3d.toe instead. Download lab-3d.zip from section 4 (ready before week 11), unzip it, and keep its model folder next to the .toe: the 360° test room lives there.

  1. Leave the engine alone

    Inside project1, CaveConfigParser cuts the picture up for the room. Don't edit it, and never press its Reload button. Its Preview button is safe.

  2. Add an object

    Press Tab, choose COMP → Geometry and place it. Rename it so it starts with geo, for example geo_first (edit the name at the top of its parameter window). It arrives with a doughnut inside, and it is now on every surface.

  3. Move it in metres

    On its Xform page set Translate to 0, 1.5, −2: eye height, 1 m in front of the front wall. Then try 0, 1.5, −3, exactly on the wall. Section 3 explains why the second one is safer.

  4. Check it in the preview

    The preview node shows your scene on a small model of the room. If it's empty, press Preview on CaveConfigParser's Custom page.

  5. Hide the test room when you don't need it

    geo1 and geo2 are the 360° test room. Turn off their Render flag to hide it, and back on to check alignment in the lab.

At home, F1 opens a giant windowF1 sends the picture to the room in the lab. On your laptop it opens a 3840 × 6480 window off the edge of your screen. Press Esc to get back.

Before you come

  • Everything is in one folder: your .toe and all images, video, sound and 3D models. With lab-3d, the model folder too.
  • Put media in the folder before you load it into TouchDesigner, so paths stay short (like media/sky.jpg). Test: rename the folder and reopen the file. Anything missing has a wrong path.
  • The folder is on a USB drive, or zipped on Google Drive. Unzipped folders take ages to download.
  • You know what you're testing. Write down three questions the room has to answer.

In the room

  1. Copy and open

    Copy your folder to the lab computer and open the .toe.

  2. Window popped up inside the room?

    The room is the computer's main display, so new windows often open there. Don't try to click them on the wall. Press Win + ← or → to move them back to the desk screen.

  3. Send it to the room

    Go to the top level (press U until you can't go higher), select the perform node and press Open as Separate Window. Or press F1 for Perform mode; Esc leaves it.

  4. Check the edges first

    Look at each surface: your image should reach every edge of its wall. With lab-3d, turn the test room on and stand at the centre: the numbered grid should run unbroken round every corner. Walk to a corner and watch it bend. Now you know where the sweet spot is.

  5. Keep the door shut

    The door is part of the left wall's picture, and any light from outside washes it out.

  6. Test from where your audience will stand

    Start at the door: that's where everyone first sees your piece. Then try other spots, not only the centre. Bring a phone to film the room for your documentation.

  7. Leave on time

    Save, copy your file back, close TouchDesigner, pack up props.

Design for blobs, not skeletonsTracking in the lab is rough. Pieces that respond to presence, position and movement survive. Pieces that need every finger tracked don't.

Sound

The lab has a 7.1 surround system, but it runs as 2.1 unless set up specially: the sound comes from the front. If sound matters, put your main image on the front wall too.
