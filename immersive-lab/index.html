1The room

The lab is a box you stand inside. Projectors cover the four walls and the floor. There is no ceiling: look up and the picture stops. Your own body casts shadows on the floor image.

Drag the model to look around. Then press Show the five cameras: the template draws each surface from one eye in the middle of the room.

The circle test

In the unfolded picture every circle is round. Fold it back and look at the front wall: the same circle becomes a tall oval. The front and back walls are 3 times wider than they are tall, but their slice of the image is 3.56 times wider. The image gets squeezed sideways to fit, so everything flat on those walls looks about 19% taller.

Fix for flat images on front and backDesign at true proportions, 3840 × 1280 (3 : 1), then resize to 3840 × 1080 before it goes in. It will look squashed on your screen and correct on the wall. Side walls and floor are close enough to leave alone. 3D scenes don't need any of this.

One pixel on the wall is about 3 mm. Think big: thin lines and small text vanish.

3The sweet spot

The template draws the whole room as seen from one eye, in the middle of the room, 1.5 m off the floor. From there, a 3D scene looks perfect, and corners disappear. Anywhere else, some things slide out of place.

Which things? Try it. This is the room from above. Drag you around the room, and drag the object (or use the buttons).

Two things are always true. From the sweet spot, everything lines up. And an object placed on a wall lines up from everywhere, because the painted picture and the object are in the same place.

The rule for group workPut what matters on the walls. Use depth for atmosphere.

Your audience will stand all over the room, so anything that floats in mid-air or sits far beyond a wall will only be right for one of them. Corners show it most: a straight line that crosses a corner bends unless you stand at the centre.

4Three ways to fill the room

The template, TDWalls_2D.toe, can fill the room in three ways. You can mix them.

A 360° picture

Wrap the room in a 360° photo or video. The template puts it on a giant sphere around the room. Out of the box, it shows the lab's test room: a numbered checkerboard.

Where
geo1 and geo2 (two copies of the same sphere), coloured by phong1 and phong2
Good for
360° photos and video, skies, found footage
Watch out
Soft. A 360° image spreads its pixels all the way round, so the front wall gets about a quarter of its sharpness.

3D objects

Build things in TouchDesigner at real sizes, in metres. Any Geometry node inside project1 whose name starts with geo appears on all five surfaces, drawn correctly for the sweet spot.

Where
Your own geo… nodes; lights must be named light…
Good for
Sculpture, architecture, anything you make
Watch out
Section 3: things away from the walls slide for anyone not at the centre.

Flat images

Paint each surface directly, like five screens. Turn the template's 3D off and plug one image (or video, or live visual) into each surface's input.

Where
CaveConfigParser, Custom page, Perspective off, then wire your TOPs into its inputs
Good for
Graphics, text, video, reactive visuals
Watch out
Make each image exactly its surface's pixel size (section 2), and pre-squash front and back.

5Where things go

Everything in the template is in metres. The centre of the floor is 0, 0, 0. A default Box in TouchDesigner is exactly 1 m on each side, so you can think in real sizes.

TouchDesigner's Translate sets the centre of an object. A 1 m box at 0, 0, 0 is half under the floor; use 0, 0.5, 0 to stand it on the floor. Anything directly above you, higher than the walls, is in the hole where the ceiling would be.

6Your first scene

Do this at home, on your own laptop, before you book the room.

  1. Install the right TouchDesigner

    Install TouchDesigner 2025, the exact build listed on Moodle, so your file opens on the lab computer. Use the Educational licence from your tutor. The free Non-Commercial licence stops at 1280 × 1280 pixels, far too small for this room.

  2. Get the template

    Download TDWalls_2D.zip from Moodle and unzip it. Keep the model folder next to the .toe file. The 360° test room lives there.

  3. Open it and look around

    Open TDWalls_2D.toe and double-click project1 to go inside. You'll find:

    CaveConfigParserThe engine that cuts the picture up for the room. Don't edit it. Never press its Reload button.
    geo1, geo2The 360° sphere (two copies of it)
    base1Makes the 360° picture from the test room model
    light1Lights your 3D objects
    previewA small 3D model of the lab showing what your walls look like

  4. Add your first object

    Press Tab, choose COMP → Geometry and place it. Rename it so it starts with geo, for example geo_first (edit the name at the top of its parameter window). It arrives with a doughnut inside, which is fine for now. It is now on every surface.

  5. Move it in metres

    In its parameters, open the Xform page and set Translate to 0, 1.5, −2: eye height, 1 m in front of the front wall. Then try 0, 1.5, −3, exactly on the wall. Section 3 explains why the second one is safer.

  6. Check it in the preview

    Look at the preview node to see your scene on a model of the room. If it's empty, select CaveConfigParser and press Preview on its Custom page. That button is safe; Reload is not.

  7. Hide the test room when you don't need it

    Turn off the Render flag on geo1 and geo2. Turn it back on to check alignment in the lab.

  8. Save into the same folder

    Save your .toe next to the model folder, and keep all your images, video and sound inside that folder too (section 7).

At home, F1 opens a giant windowF1 sends the picture to the room in the lab. On your laptop it opens a 3840 × 6480 window off the edge of your screen. Press Esc to get back.

Before you come

  • Everything is in one folder: your .toe, the model folder, and all images, video, sound and 3D models.
  • Put media in the folder before you load it into TouchDesigner, so paths stay short (like media/sky.jpg). Test: rename the folder and reopen the file. Anything missing has a wrong path.
  • The folder is on a USB drive, or zipped on Google Drive. Unzipped folders take ages to download.
  • You know what you're testing. Write down three questions the room has to answer.

In the room

  1. Copy and open

    Copy your folder to the lab computer and open the .toe.

  2. Window popped up inside the room?

    The room is the computer's main display, so new windows often open there. Don't try to click them on the wall. Press Win + ← or → to move them back to the desk screen.

  3. Send it to the room

    Go to the top level (press U until you can't go higher), select the perform node and press Open as Separate Window. Or press F1 for Perform mode; Esc leaves it.

  4. Check the alignment first

    Turn the test room on. Stand at the centre: the numbered grid should run unbroken round every corner. Walk to a corner and watch it bend. Now you know where the sweet spot is.

  5. Test from where your audience will stand

    Not only from the centre. Bring a phone to film the room for your documentation.

  6. Leave on time

    Save, copy your file back, close TouchDesigner, pack up props.

Sound

The lab has a 7.1 surround system, but it runs as 2.1 unless set up specially: the sound comes from the front. If sound matters, put your main image on the front wall too.
